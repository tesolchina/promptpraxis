TEXT: Part 1 · A hundred open tabs
TEXT: In July 2022, Maya Gosztyla wrote in the Nature Career Column about the evening her laptop died. More than a hundred papers were waiting in browser tabs, each a promise to read later. When the crash took them, her first feeling was relief: the loss forced a decision she had been postponing.1
TEXT: Her article set out four steps: locate the relevant research literature, curate the literature using Zotero, read the papers on a set routine, and organize your notes in a database you can query. Locating means finding the journal articles that matter to the researcher; curating means turning those finds into a lasting indexed database. This entry takes the first two; reading and organizing remain human work, since no script supplies the critical judgement they demand. Entry 3 picks up steps three and four.

Agentic AI builds on them. With vibe coding we can write small tools that reproduce these workflows, or adopt newer tools designed to work with agents.

TEXT: Step 1: Locate the relevant research literature
TEXT: Stop hunting for new papers and let them come to you. Really Simple Syndication (RSS) is a web feed format that carries updates from websites in a standard, computer-readable structure: each feed document lists new items with a title, a summary and a link, and is republished whenever the source adds something. A reader program follows those addresses and gathers everything you subscribe to into one place, removing the need to check each journal by hand; the same mechanism carries blog posts, news headlines and podcast episodes well beyond academic literature. What you can then do with the stream depends on the reader you choose: a typical reader lets you skim the titles, mark what you have seen and save items for later, while filtering, full-text reading and search vary from reader to reader. The three steps below set up a basic reading routine.
NOTE: Handwritten mark circling the colon after "choose" and drawing an arrow to a handwritten semicolon in the right margin.
TEXT: 1 Choose a reader. Web readers such as Feedly or Inoreader need no installation; desktop and mobile readers work offline.
TEXT: 2 Subscribe with feed addresses. Paste verified URLs into your reader: Nature, Nature Communications, arXiv cs.AI.
TEXT: 3 Keep a five-minute morning routine. Scan titles with your tea, move real candidates to Read Later, close the reader.

TEXT: Part 5 · What we chose to cover, what can go wrong, and why upgrading beats rebuilding

TEXT: We limit the lesson to find and manage; an entry attempting everything teaches nothing, and BibTeX/RIS export stays the dependable bridge to reference managers. Language models can invent or miscopy citations even when tools are available, so compare outputs with the saved API response, keep missing fields empty, and judge relevance yourself — a DOI identifies a record; it does not establish the quality of its claims or guarantee free full text, and API conditions change, so stop on errors.2 What remains is the payoff: an afternoon of tab chasing becomes a two-minute query. Next month’s review is this month’s prompt with a new date filter.
